Understanding Flock Fiber Specifications: Denier, Length, and Color Fastness

Published: August 13, 2026 | By Fenxing Flock Team

When you request a quote for flock fiber, suppliers send back a spec sheet — a short list of numbers like "3.0D × 1.0mm, color fastness 4". If you don't know how to read it, you're buying blind. This guide explains the three most important parameters — denier, cut length, and color fastness — plus a few others you should check before you place an order.

Denier: How Thick Is the Fiber?

Denier (D) measures fiber thickness. Technically, 1 denier means 9,000 meters of the fiber weighs 1 gram — so a lower denier number means a thinner, finer fiber. This single number has a big effect on how your finished product feels.

Some suppliers (especially European ones) quote thickness in dtex instead of denier — 1 denier ≈ 1.11 dtex. Flock fibers span from ultra-fine 0.3D up to coarse 30D and beyond for specialized uses.

Denier RangeFeel / FinishTypical Applications
0.5D – 3D (fine)Very soft, dense, velvetyApparel, home textiles, jewelry boxes
3D – 6D (medium)Balanced softness and textureGeneral flocking, packaging, display
6D – 30D (coarse)Rough, textured, hard-wearingAutomotive interiors, carpets, industrial seals
  • Rule of thumb: finer denier = softer, denser, more premium feel; coarser denier = more durable and textured.
  • Cost note: finer fibers are generally more expensive because they take longer to produce and are harder to flock uniformly.
  • Watch out: fibers that are too fine (below ~1.5D) can clump in humid conditions, so anti-static treatment is recommended.

Cut Length: How Long Are the Fibers?

Cut length is the length of each individual fiber after it's been cut from the filament. It controls the "pile height" of your flocked surface — how much the fibers stand up. Standard flocking uses 0.3–3.0mm; longer fibers up to 5.0mm (or more for specialty applications) create a plush, fur-like finish.

Cut LengthResulting TextureBest For
0.3 – 0.7mmFine, powder-like, subtleDetailed logos, thin lines, fine printing
0.7 – 1.5mmStandard velour feelMost general flocking work
1.5 – 3.0mm+Long, plush, fuzzyDecorative items, toys, soft-touch panels
  • Key principle: fiber length should always be greater than fiber diameter, or the fibers can't stand upright during electrostatic flocking.
  • Match to your adhesive: longer fibers need a thicker adhesive film to anchor them securely.

Color Fastness: How Long Will the Color Last?

Color fastness rates how well the dye resists fading, rubbing off, or running. Rubbing (crocking) and washing fastness are graded 1–5 under AATCC/ISO test methods, while light fastness uses the Blue Wool Scale of 1–8. In every case, a higher grade means better color retention.

TestWhat It ChecksWhy Buyers Care
Rubbing / CrockingColor transfers under frictionCritical for upholstery and apparel
WashingColor bleeds in waterImportant for washable textiles
Light — Blue Wool Scale (1–8)Fading under sunlight/UVKey for outdoor or window-exposed items
SublimationDye evaporates under heatRelevant for automotive (high heat)
  • By material: polyester generally has the best color fastness, nylon is good, and viscose rayon is the most sensitive to water and light.
  • What to ask for: the ASTM performance spec for flocked upholstery requires at least grade 4 dry / grade 3 wet crocking. For heavy-use products, aim for grade 4 or higher on both.

Other Parameters Worth Checking

  • Fiber type: nylon (PA), viscose rayon, polyester (PET), or modified polyester — this is the biggest driver of feel, durability, and price.
  • Anti-static treatment: essential for fine fibers, which otherwise clump during electrostatic flocking.
  • Moisture content: affects storage life and electrostatic performance; look for 0.5%–4% depending on material.
  • Cross-section shape: round fibers are standard; special shapes (e.g. trilobal) create unique optical effects.

Frequently Asked Questions

What denier is best for a soft, premium feel?
For a velvety, luxurious touch, choose a fine denier in the 1.5D–3D range. Viscose rayon in fine deniers is the softest option for textiles.
Does cut length affect how dense the flocking looks?
Not directly — density is mostly determined by denier and flocking technique. Cut length controls pile height (how long the fibers stand up), not how tightly packed they are.
What color fastness grade should I require?
For products that face regular use or washing, require grade 4 or higher on rubbing and washing tests. For outdoor use, also check the light-fastness rating.
Why does polyester have better color fastness than viscose rayon?
Polyester fibers are dyed at high temperature during production, which bonds the dye strongly. Viscose rayon is a regenerated cellulose fiber that absorbs moisture and releases dye more easily.
