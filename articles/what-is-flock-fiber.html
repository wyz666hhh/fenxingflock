What is Flock Fiber? A Complete Guide to Types, Uses, and Selection

Published: August 14, 2026 | By Fenxing Flock Team

Flock fiber is a very short, finely cut fiber — typically 0.3 mm to 5 mm long — that is applied to a surface to create a soft, velvet-like finish. The process, called electrostatic flocking, uses an electric field to stand millions of tiny fibers upright and embed them vertically into an adhesive-coated surface. The result is a dense, uniform "flocked" texture you have almost certainly touched: the felt-like lining of a jewelry box, the soft seal inside a car door, or the printed logo on a T-shirt.

How Electrostatic Flocking Works

Flocking is a three-step process, and understanding it makes it much easier to choose the right fiber for your product.

  1. Coating. An adhesive (usually water-based acrylic or PU) is applied to the substrate — fabric, plastic, metal, rubber, or wood.
  2. Flocking. The surface passes through or under an electrostatic field. Each fiber becomes charged and is attracted toward the grounded substrate, so it lands standing straight up rather than lying flat.
  3. Curing & cleaning. The adhesive is cured, then loose, excess fibers are vacuumed away, leaving only the densely packed, upright fibers.

Because the fibers stand on end, flocked surfaces feel soft and thick even though the actual coating is only a fraction of a millimeter deep. This upright orientation is what separates true flocking from simply sprinkling fibers onto glue.

The Three Main Types of Flock Fiber

Nearly all flock fiber falls into one of three material families. Each behaves differently, so the "best" one depends entirely on your application.

Nylon (Polyamide) Flock Fiber

Nylon is the premium, workhorse material of the industry. It offers the best abrasion resistance, heat resistance (up to 180°C), and electrostatic performance, which is why it dominates automotive and industrial applications. If a flocked part will be rubbed, flexed, or exposed to weather, nylon is usually the safest choice.

Viscose Rayon Flock Fiber

Viscose Rayon is the traditional textile choice and remains the softest fiber available. It produces a rich, velvety drape that consumers associate with luxury, making it the default for fashion, upholstery, and decorative flocking. Its main limitation is moisture sensitivity — it is not suitable for wet or high-humidity environments.

Polyester Flock Fiber

Polyester is the economical all-rounder. It holds color exceptionally well and resists moisture, at the lowest price point of the three. It is the go-to for high-volume, cost-driven projects like packaging inserts and promotional products. For a deeper comparison of these materials, see our nylon vs viscose rayon vs polyester guide.

Two Specifications That Matter Most

When you compare flock fiber from different suppliers, two numbers tell you most of what you need to know:

  • Denier (D) — fiber fineness. Lower denier (1.5D–3D) produces a finer, denser, softer finish; higher denier (6D–30D) creates a coarser, more textured feel.
  • Cut Length (mm) — fiber length. Shorter fibers (0.3–0.5 mm) give a smooth, suede-like surface; longer fibers (1.0–5.0 mm) give a deeper, plush pile.

Where Is Flock Fiber Used?

Flocking appears in more products than most people realize. The most common applications include:

  • Automotive: window channels, glove boxes, door seals, and dashboard components that need a soft, rattle-free, wear-resistant surface.
  • Home textiles & furniture: upholstery, curtains, wallpaper, and decorative trim.
  • Apparel: T-shirt logos, flocked fabrics, and heat-transfer designs.
  • Packaging & display: jewelry boxes, cosmetic cases, and premium packaging inserts.
  • Industrial: EPDM rubber profiles, seals, and gaskets.

How to Choose the Right Flock Fiber

Work through these four questions in order, and the material choice usually becomes obvious:

  1. Where will the product be used? Outdoor or high-wear → nylon. Indoor, low-wear, decorative → viscose or polyester.
  2. What feel do you want? The softest possible → viscose rayon. Balanced softness and strength → nylon.
  3. What is your volume and cost target? Tight budget, high volume → polyester.
  4. Any special requirements? Color fastness under UV → polyester or nylon. Moisture resistance → avoid viscose.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is flock fiber made of?
Most flock fiber is made from nylon (polyamide), viscose rayon, or polyester. Nylon is the most durable, viscose rayon is the softest, and polyester is the most economical.
What is the difference between flock and velvet?
Both have a soft, raised surface, but flock is created by electrostatically planting short fibers onto a coated surface, while velvet is a woven or knitted fabric. Flocking can be applied directly to almost any substrate.
How thick is a flocked coating?
Typically 0.3 mm to 5 mm, depending on fiber length. Despite the soft feel, the actual coating is very thin and lightweight.
Can I get flock fiber in custom colors?
Yes. Flock fiber is dyed to match, and we offer color matching across a wide range of shades, including custom formulations.
