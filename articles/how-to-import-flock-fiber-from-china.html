How to Import Flock Fiber from China: A Step-by-Step Guide for Buyers

Published: August 11, 2026 | By Fenxing Flock Team

China produces the vast majority of the world's flock fiber — by some estimates, over 70% of global supply. For international buyers, importing directly from Chinese manufacturers offers significant cost advantages, but the process can feel intimidating if you've never done it before. This guide walks you through every step, from finding a reliable supplier to clearing your shipment through customs.

Step 2: Request and Evaluate Samples

Never place a bulk order without testing samples first. Here's what to do:

  1. Request samples of each material type you're considering — nylon, viscose, and polyester, in the denier and cut length you need. Most suppliers will send small sample packs (50–200g per type) for free or at minimal cost; you typically cover the shipping.
  2. Check physical properties: Feel the softness, examine color consistency under good lighting, and check for excessive powder or broken fibers (a sign of poor cutting quality).
  3. Run a test flock: If you have flocking equipment, apply each sample to your actual substrate. Evaluate electrostatic performance, coverage density, and final appearance.
  4. Compare multiple suppliers: Always sample from at least 2–3 suppliers. Different factories produce noticeably different fiber quality even with the same specifications on paper.

What affects pricing? Material type (nylon > viscose > polyester), denier (finer is generally more expensive), dyeing requirements, and seasonal demand fluctuations. Prices also vary by region — manufacturers in Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces (the traditional textile heartland) may have different pricing than those in Guangdong.

Incoterms to know: FOB (Free on Board) means the supplier covers all costs until the goods are loaded onto the ship — this is the most common arrangement for first-time buyers. CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) means the supplier arranges shipping to your destination port. EXW (Ex Works) is cheapest but places all logistics responsibility on you.

Step 5: Handle Customs Clearance

Flock fiber falls under textile fiber HS codes. The exact code depends on the material:

  • Nylon flock fiber: HS Code 5601.30 (textile flock and dust, of man-made fibers)
  • Viscose Rayon flock fiber: HS Code 5601.30 (same heading, different material subcategory)
  • Polyester flock fiber: HS Code 5601.30

Documents you'll typically need:

  • Commercial Invoice — listing product description, quantity, unit price, total value
  • Packing List — detailed breakdown of packages, weights, dimensions
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) — for sea freight; or Air Waybill for air freight
  • Certificate of Origin — may be required depending on your country's trade agreements with China
  • MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet) — flock fiber is generally non-hazardous, but some adhesives or treated fibers may require this

Import duties: Check your country's tariff schedule for HS 5601.30. Many countries apply standard textile duties (typically 5–15%). Some developing nations have preferential rates for Chinese textile imports under bilateral trade agreements.

Step 6: Quality Inspection — Before You Pay the Balance

Smart buyers inspect before the final payment. You have several options:

  1. Pre-shipment inspection by a third party: Companies like SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek will visit the factory, inspect your goods, and send you a detailed report — typically costing $300–500 per inspection. This is the gold standard for orders over $5,000.
  2. Request photos and videos: Ask the supplier to photograph each package, label, and a close-up of the fiber before shipping. Reputable suppliers do this proactively.
  3. Agree on a quality standard upfront: Specify acceptable tolerances in your purchase contract — e.g., denier ±5%, length variation under 10%, moisture content under 3%.
  4. Payment terms that protect you: Common structure is 30% deposit to start production, 70% balance after inspection and before shipping. Never pay 100% upfront with a new supplier.

Common Pitfalls — and How to Avoid Them

🛑 Pitfall 1: "The sample was great, but the bulk order was different."
This is the most common complaint in the flock fiber trade. Some factories send carefully prepared samples but cut corners in production. Solution: Specify in the contract that bulk quality must match the approved sample. Include a penalty clause for non-conformance. Consider third-party inspection.

🛑 Pitfall 2: Hidden shipping costs.
The quoted FOB price looks attractive, but you didn't account for freight, insurance, port charges, customs brokerage, and last-mile delivery. Solution: Ask your supplier for a CIF quote alongside the FOB quote so you can see the total difference. Get quotes from your local freight forwarder for the destination-side costs before committing.

🛑 Pitfall 3: Miscommunication about specifications.
"Denier" and "Dtex" are sometimes used interchangeably — but they're different units (1 Dtex = 0.9 Denier). A "3D" nylon fiber is not the same as a "3 Dtex" fiber. Solution: Always confirm units in writing. Use photos and reference samples as the ultimate standard — "bulk must match this sample" is clearer than any spec sheet.

🛑 Pitfall 4: Choosing the cheapest quote without due diligence.
A price 30% below market average is a red flag, not a bargain. Solution: If a price seems too good to be true, ask why. Request their factory address (verify it exists on maps), business license, and references from existing international clients.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to ship flock fiber internationally?
For small samples (under 5 kg), express shipping costs $30–80 depending on destination. For bulk orders by sea, shipping typically adds $0.30–1.00 per kg to your product cost — but this varies dramatically by route and fuel surcharges. Always get a freight quote for your specific origin-destination pair.
Do I need a customs broker to import flock fiber?
For commercial quantities, yes — a licensed customs broker in your country will handle the paperwork, calculate duties, and ensure compliance. For small sample shipments via courier, the courier company (DHL, FedEx, etc.) typically handles clearance as part of their service.
What's the minimum order quantity (MOQ) for flock fiber from China?
Most manufacturers set MOQs between 50–200 kg per color per material type. Some will do smaller trial orders (10–30 kg) for first-time buyers at a modest premium. We at Fenxing Flock offer flexible MOQs for new customers — contact us to discuss your needs.
How long does production take after placing an order?
For standard colors and specifications, production typically takes 7–14 days. Custom-dyed colors add 5–10 days. During peak season (September–December), lead times may extend by 1–2 weeks. Always confirm the current production schedule with your supplier before placing a time-sensitive order.
Can I visit the factory before placing an order?
Absolutely — and we encourage it. A factory visit lets you see production capabilities, quality control processes, and raw material storage conditions firsthand. Most suppliers in Foshan and the Pearl River Delta are within 1–2 hours of Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN).
